## WHAT THE COMPS AGREE ON

- **The teller line is the room.** In 1, 2, 4, 6 and 7 the single strongest shape is one long counter that divides
  public from staff: a solid lower front (marble, wood veneer or white laminate) and a screen above it (brass grilles,
  glass, or a bandit screen to the ceiling), windows at regular pitch, each with a deal tray. It is straight, long and
  repeated, and it is the cover line a fight happens across.
- **A check desk island** stands mid-floor in front of it (1, 4, 7): a standing-height table with slots for slips.
- **Bright, even ceilings.** Every comp after the 1920s one is lit by fluorescents in a regular grid: lay-in troffers
  (2, 7), long linear runs (3, 4, 8, 10), suspended linear fittings (6). The 1920s hall hangs globes on rods. Nothing
  is moody. A bank is the opposite of the club and the dive bar.
- **Hard, pale floors in public, carpet behind.** Terrazzo (2, 4), beige tile (5, 6, 7), with carpet where staff sit
  (3, 5) and rug runners at the line (7).
- **Wood and one accent colour.** Dark wood veneer on the counter fronts and panelling (2, 4, 7), against white or
  beige walls. The 1990s branches add one corporate colour (a blue band in 5 and 6, a burgundy bulkhead in 7).
- **Queue furniture and paper.** Rope stanchions (6), poster stands and leaflet racks (6), a date clock over the line
  (2, 4), a wall clock, plants in planters (1, 4, 5, 6).
- **Behind the line it is an office:** desks with CRTs, printers, phones, filing and cables (3, 5, 8, 9).
- **The money is in hard rooms:** steel counting tables and bags (10), cages and carts (11), deposit boxes against
  glazed brick (12), all lit by bare tubes, all steel and concrete.

## A 1990s Delaware County bank, researched (2026-10-27)

The walker asked "were there cctv cameras in the 90s?" and "what else should a bank have from the 90s in PA?", and
then "dig in". Read from the sources below; a claim marked UNCONFIRMED was not found in them.

- **Cameras, yes, and old by then.**
  - The Bank Protection Act of 1968 asks banks for devices that "assist in the identification and prosecution" of
    robbers, and most met that with interior cameras.
  - By the 1990s these were boxy CCTV cameras on brackets, recording to time-lapse VCRs; a 1999 New York
    regulator's letter covers how often the tapes may be reused. VCRs gave way to DVRs only later.
  - Placement was "the bank floor and teller areas" and the ATM; few faced the parking lot.
  - Comp 6 has one on its column. The library has the species (Zoo `security_camera`), and no bank places one: each
    carries one gameplay `camera_socket` marker and no visible camera.
- **The bandit barrier.** Bullet-resistant glass at the teller line, priced at $20,000-25,000 a branch in 1997, and
  later urged floor to ceiling. Comp 6's full-height glass is one.
- **The MAC machine: the Philadelphia ATM.**
  - MAC (Money Access Center) was the region's shared ATM network, started by Philadelphia National Bank. Locals
    called any ATM a "MAC machine".
  - In 1996 the big banks added $1 surcharges on them.
  - A real network mark: the factory's ATM keeps its invented brand. A sticker in the spirit (a network logo on the
    ATM's fascia) is fair.
- **The banks of the decade:** CoreStates (from Philadelphia National Bank), Meridian (taken by CoreStates in 1995),
  Mellon PSFS, PNC, Fidelity, Commerce, then First Union (CoreStates, 1998), all merging and closing branches. A 1990s
  Delco branch is a brand that changed twice. Real names, so the factory's bank names stay invented
  (`zoo/core/brands.py` and its siblings).
- **Commerce's "Penny Arcade" coin counters:** free coin counting machines in the lobby, rolled into its New Jersey
  branches in the late 1990s. Philadelphia branches had them by 2003; in a 1999 Delco branch they are UNCONFIRMED.
- **The lobby rate board:** the CD and savings rates posted inside, which a 1992 law let carry only the APY. Lobby
  posters opened the CHRISTMAS CLUB season, a Depression-era holiday savings account still sold.
- **The drive-up and the night drop.**
  - A drive-up lane with a pneumatic tube (the canister to the teller and back) was common from the 1960s and
    dwindled only with the ATM.
  - A night depository is a slot in the wall for after-hours deposits: envelopes for people, locking zip bags for
    businesses, collected the next morning.
- **Time and temperature.** A bank's street sign that flashed the time and the temperature, from 1951 on, a way to
  draw the eye at night in a small town. Common on 1990s branches. UNCONFIRMED for Delco in particular.
