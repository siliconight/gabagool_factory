# Curb ramps at crosswalks: what the rules say, and what a 1997 street had

Read for roadmap 244 (a marked crosswalk that lands on a raised kerb), 2026-10-13. Read before any number
is written down, as `STREET_DRAINS.md` was. The game's streets are Philadelphia and Delaware County in
1997, a fictional world: a reference fills what the walker's comps leave open and overrides nothing they
show (`references-fill-blanks-not-override`).

## What a ramp is, by the current federal rule

The Access Board's public right-of-way guidelines (PROWAG, final rule 2023), as reproduced by state
training material (FDOT, MnDOT, NJDOT 2024, the CP Tech Center's 2023 presentation, the Access Board's own
training slides):

- **A curb ramp or a blended transition for each crosswalk, marked or unmarked** (NJDOT's 2024 PROWAG
  training).
- **Running slope 1:12 (8.3 %) at most** (R304.2.1); where 1:12 would need a run over 15 ft, 15 ft at
  any slope. Some states hold 8.3 % with no construction tolerance (Indiana); FDOT recommends 7.1 %.
- **Cross slope 1:48 (2.1 %) at most** (R304.2.2).
- **48 in (1.22 m) wide at least**, the pedestrian access route's width.
- **A 48 x 48 in clear area beyond the bottom grade break**, within the crosswalk's width, at most 1:20,
  and wholly outside the travel lanes (R304.2.4).
- **A turning space at the top**, 4 x 4 ft minimum (the Access Board's slides; Bend, Oregon draws 5 x 5).

## What a 1997 Philadelphia street had

**Kinney v. Yerusalim, 9 F.3d 1067 (3d Cir. 1993)**, cert. denied 1994:

- The city's practice had been to put curb ramps on a sidewalk only where work on a street directly
  touched the kerb or sidewalk, or where the street was rebuilt whole.
- The court held that resurfacing a street, intersection to intersection, is an "alteration" under the
  ADA's regulation (28 C.F.R. 35.151(e)(1)).
- So Philadelphia had to install curb ramps at the intersections of every street resurfaced since
  January 26, 1992. A supplemental order in 1994 required them at the time of the resurfacing.

(Read from case summaries and the court's citation pages, not the full opinion.)

**So in 1997 a marked crosswalk could meet a full kerb**, on a street not resurfaced since 1992. Ramps
stood at the corners of the streets that had been, and the gap was still being litigated in 2019 (the
Disability Rights Advocates' Philadelphia sidewalks complaint). Neither source gives a share of 1997
corners with ramps.

- Ramps at **every** crosswalk (today's rule), at **none** (a street nobody had resurfaced), or **by
  street** -- a street resurfaced since 1992 ramped at both ends of each walk, an older one not. That
  is the period's own mix, and it reads as a cause rather than as noise.
