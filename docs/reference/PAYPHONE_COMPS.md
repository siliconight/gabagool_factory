# Payphones: the walker's comps, and what each tool owes them

The walker, 2026-10-08:

> Another roadmap item for city/urban levels: PayPhones
>
> In the 90s you would pay with quarters, and the phone is not wireless,
> it's on a metal cord.

Three photographs came with it. They were shown in chat and are not stored
here. What follows is what was read off them -- format only. The telephone
companies' marks on the booths and stickers are real, and none is reproduced.
Roadmap 210 tracks the work.

## WHAT A 1990s PAYPHONE IS

- **The instrument.** A stainless-steel coin phone:
  - a 12-key keypad, and a coin slot high on the face (quarters);
  - a coin-return door low down, and the coin box's locked vault door under
    it;
  - a hook-switch cradle at one side for the handset;
  - instruction and number cards behind clear plastic.
- **The cord.** The handset hangs on an armoured steel cord, a flexible
  stainless sleeve. It loops from the instrument's side down below the shelf
  and back up. It is never a curly plastic cord, and never wireless.
- **The enclosure**, in three kinds:
  1. **A booth on a post.** A flat roof with a header sign reading "Phone"
     beside a telephone company's mark, two side panels, and the instrument
     on a back panel.
  2. **A pedestal shroud.** A blue painted enclosure on a square black post,
     open at the front, with handset pictograms stamped into its side panels.
  3. **A wall shroud.** The same enclosure bolted to a building's wall
     (white-painted brick in the comp), with a "Phone" header and a
     phone-book binder hanging under it.
- **The wear.** Graffiti tags on the shroud, stickers on the instrument,
  scratched paint, and a dented binder.

## WHAT EXISTS TODAY (read 2026-10-08)

- **Zoo's `payphone`** (roadmap 153, the 1990s street kit) is a half-booth
  on a pedestal, all boxes in an 81-line recipe: a post, a back panel, a
  hood, a face plate, a coin box, and a handset on a hook. It has no cord,
  no keypad and no coin slot or coin return. Its genome lists no parts and
  no params, and carries styles `default`, `delco`, `center_city`,
  `industrial_flats` and `rockay`.
- **Lot places exactly one**, at a bus stop: `site_furniture._stop_corner`
  stands it 6.4 m along from the shelter, beside the mailbox and the news
  racks. A street with no stop has no payphone.

## WHAT EACH TOOL OWES IT (to be confirmed against the code)

- **Zoo: the instrument and its cord.**
  - **The instrument, redrawn.** The keypad, the coin slot and coin return,
    the cradle, the cards and the vault door.
  - **The cord.** An armoured steel cord as a swept tube on a hanging
    curve, with the handset in its cradle.
  - **The enclosure as forms:** booth, pedestal and wall.
  - **The trim.** A "PHONE" header carrying an invented telephone company,
    a phone-book binder, graffiti and stickers.
  - **Materials.** A painted shroud carrying the genome's colour, and a
    stainless instrument, merged by material.
- **Lot: payphones beyond the bus stop**, on the streets the walker means by
  city and urban levels. Wall shrouds go on store walls by the door; a
  pedestal goes at a corner.
- **The name.** An invented Delco telephone company under the fake-brands
  rule, never a real carrier's mark.
