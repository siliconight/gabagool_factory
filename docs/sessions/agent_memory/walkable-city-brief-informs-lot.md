The walker, 2026-10-07, mid-way through the night-lighting work: "I know we
are focused on lighting right now, please continue, but this should be placed
and inform future Lot layout".

Filed byte-identical at `docs/reference/DYNAMIC_WALKABLE_CITY_LEVEL_DESIGN_BRIEF.md`
(21,123 bytes). It sits beside its named companion,
`LAND_PRESSURE_AND_SPATIAL_LOGIC.md`, the land-use guide Lot already builds
to ([[land-use-guide-informs-lot]]).

What it asks of a layout:
- **Hub and spokes:** the score target central in movement, not geometry.
- **At least three distinct approaches**, each with its own exposure,
  distance, height or access. No cosmetic duplicate spokes.
- **Cross-links and loops:**
  - a short loop near the hub;
  - a long route around the most exposed area;
  - a shortcut with a readable cost;
  - more than one recovery from a blocked route.
- **Dead ends only with a purpose:** a loading yard, a fenced lot.
- **Edges explained:** a road continues, a wall, a rail line, a grade.
  Never a clipped grid.
- **Verticality with a reason**, joining real route nodes at both ends.
- **Adjacency with a reason:** the shared customers or deliveries tables.
- **A landmark hierarchy:** primary, secondary, local. Reveal in sequence.
- **A planning order:** site, district, anchor, movement graph, parcels,
  neighbours, massing, history, dressing, review.
- **Validation:** hard failures, design warnings, and a traversal review of
  the fastest, safest, elevated, service and blocked-edge routes.

**Not started.** Lot's layout has not been compared to it.

**How to apply:** when Lot layout work resumes, start by mapping the brief's
hard failures and design warnings onto the gates Lot, Level Factory and Laser
Tag already run. Then measure the gap before changing layout. Lighting came
first that day by the walker's word.
